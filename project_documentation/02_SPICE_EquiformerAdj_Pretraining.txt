02_SPICE_EquiformerAdj_Pretraining
==================================

Purpose
-------

We added a separate pretraining stage for the adjacency-aware Equiformer. The
goal is to learn a chemically meaningful 3D encoder before using it for
pharmacophore feature representation and matching.

This training stage is independent of the QM9 benchmarking experiments. QM9 is
still useful for comparing molecular representation models and property
prediction, but the SPICE experiment has a different purpose: it trains the
EquiformerAdj core directly from quantum-mechanical energies and forces.


Why We Chose SPICE
------------------

We chose SPICE because it connects the quantum-chemical and pharmacophore parts
of the project particularly well.

SPICE contains drug-like molecules, peptides, dimers, and solvated amino-acid
systems. For every conformation used here, it provides:

- atomic numbers, which identify the chemical elements;
- three-dimensional atomic coordinates;
- quantum-mechanical formation energies;
- DFT energy gradients, which we convert to atomic forces;
- systems containing H, Li, B, C, N, O, F, Na, Mg, Si, P, S, Cl, K, Ca, Br,
  and I in SPICE 2.0.1.

This elemental coverage is important for pharmacophore screening. Common
pharmacophore patterns depend on atoms and chemical environments such as
nitrogen and oxygen in hydrogen-bond donors or acceptors, aromatic carbon
systems, sulfur-containing groups, phosphorus-containing groups, and halogens.
The 3D coordinates are equally important because a pharmacophore is defined not
only by which features exist, but also by their spatial arrangement.

SPICE does not contain ready-made pharmacophore labels. We later obtain those
features with RDKit and connect them to the atoms that define each feature. The
reason SPICE is valuable at the pretraining stage is that it teaches the encoder
about atomic identity, molecular geometry, intramolecular interactions, and
intermolecular interactions through quantum-mechanical supervision. We can then
fine-tune the learned geometric representation for pharmacophore extraction and
matching instead of starting from randomly initialized weights.

The SPICE calculations were performed at the
ωB97M-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVPPD level of theory. The dataset is therefore relevant to
the non-covalent and geometry-dependent interactions that matter in molecular
recognition.


Separation from the QM9 Benchmark
---------------------------------

The two experiments answer different questions:

- QM9 benchmarking asks which model family performs well for molecular property
  prediction on small molecules.
- SPICE pretraining asks whether EquiformerAdj can learn a transferable 3D
  molecular representation from quantum-mechanical energies and forces.

The SPICE implementation is kept under:

pharm_training/

It does not import the QM9 training scripts. This separation prevents benchmark
settings, targets, and checkpoints from being mixed with the SPICE experiment.
The pharmacophore adapter for this workflow is also kept in this folder:

pharm_training/equiformer_encoder_pharmaco_feat.py

It is separate from the older QM9 benchmarking encoders under
benchmarking/Methods/.


Dataset Download and Preparation
--------------------------------

Create and activate the project environment first:

conda env create -f environment.yml
conda activate equipharm

Download the official SPICE 2.0.1 HDF5 file:

bash scripts/download_datasets.sh spice

The downloader stores it at:

data/SPICE/SPICE-2.0.1.hdf5

It supports resumed downloads and verifies the downloaded file with the
published checksum.

Prepare the training manifest:

python -m pharm_training.prepare_spice

Preparation does not copy the large HDF5 data. It validates the available
groups and writes:

data/SPICE/prepared/manifest.json

The manifest contains molecule-disjoint 90%/5%/5% training, validation, and test
splits. All conformations belonging to one molecular group remain in the same
split. This avoids an easy form of data leakage in which nearly identical
conformations of one molecule occur in both training and validation data.


Units and Training Targets
--------------------------

SPICE coordinates are stored in Bohr and are converted to Angstrom:

position_A = position_bohr * 0.529177210903

Formation energies are converted from Hartree to electronvolt:

energy_eV = energy_hartree * 27.211386245988

SPICE stores the DFT total gradient. Force is the negative energy gradient:

force = -dE/dr

After the unit conversion, force is expressed in eV/Angstrom.

The network predicts one total energy per molecular conformation from invariant
degree-0 features. It predicts one 3D atomic force from equivariant degree-1
features. A learned linear combination across degree-1 channels remains a 3D
equivariant vector, so rotating the molecule rotates the predicted forces.

We originally calculated predicted forces as the negative coordinate gradient
of predicted energy. Training against those forces required second derivatives
through the coordinate rotations used by equiformer-pytorch. In practice, this
produced finite losses followed by non-finite parameter-gradient norms. The
documented workflow therefore uses force_mode = direct. It keeps force
supervision and stable first-order backpropagation, but it does not constrain
the direct force prediction to equal the energy gradient.

The energy_gradient mode remains available as an experimental option for a
backend or numerical precision that supports stable second derivatives. It is
not used by the default SPICE search.


Graph Construction
------------------

For each conformation, we calculate pairwise atomic distances and connect atoms
within a 6 Angstrom cutoff. The default EquiformerAdj configuration uses:

- hidden dimension: 128
- depth: 6 Equiformer blocks
- scalar input degree and scalar/vector internal representations
- attention heads: 4
- geometric neighbors added outside adjacency: 0
- adjacency-degree embedding: 2
- default sparse-neighbor cap: 32
- self-attention: enabled

Setting num_neighbors to zero is deliberate. The graph already represents the
6 Angstrom neighborhood, so adding extra nearest atoms could silently change the
meaning of the cutoff. The sparse-neighbor cap is included in the hyperparameter
search because a cap that is too small can remove useful local interactions,
while a high cap increases memory use.

The categorical element field is named atom_type in each PyG Data object. It
must not be named element_index: PyTorch Geometric automatically increments
batched attributes containing the word "index", because it assumes that they
refer to graph-node indices. Incrementing an element category would produce
values outside the 17-element embedding table. The encoder validates the
atom_type range before performing the embedding lookup.


Loss Function
-------------

The energy error is divided by the number of atoms in each molecule before the
loss is calculated. This makes energy errors from differently sized SPICE
systems more comparable.

The training objective is:

training_loss = energy_weight * SmoothL1(energy_error_per_atom)
              + force_weight  * SmoothL1(force_error)

The default starting weights are:

- energy weight: 1
- force weight: 50

Force weight is treated as a hyperparameter rather than a fixed scientific
constant. SPICE provides three force components per atom, so force supervision
contains much more information per conformation than the single energy value.
Testing different force weights allows us to balance energy accuracy and local
geometric accuracy empirically.


Validation Score and Model Selection
------------------------------------

We do not rank hyperparameter trials by their weighted training loss. That would
be unfair because changing force_weight also changes the numerical scale of the
loss.

Every trial is ranked with the same normalized validation score:

validation_score = 0.5 * energy_MAE_eV_per_atom / 0.043
                 + 0.5 * force_MAE_eV_per_Angstrom / 0.10

Lower is better. The fixed reference scales make energy and force errors
comparable without allowing one trial to benefit simply from a different loss
weight.

The individual validation energy MAE and force MAE are always saved alongside
this combined score. We therefore keep the underlying scientific errors visible
instead of reporting only one aggregate number.

The test split is not evaluated during hyperparameter optimization. It is used
only after the winning validation configuration has been selected and promoted
to the full training run.


Hyperparameter Optimization
---------------------------

The search controller is:

pharm_training/search.py

Its configuration is:

pharm_training/configs/spice_search.json

The pilot search tests combinations of:

- learning rate: 1e-4, 2e-4, and 5e-4;
- force weight: 10, 50, and 100;
- weight decay: 1e-6 and 1e-5;
- maximum sparse neighbors: 16, 24, and 32.

The complete Cartesian grid contains 54 combinations. The current pilot budget
selects 18 combinations deterministically with seed 42. This keeps the first
search computationally manageable while still exploring different optimization
and graph-density settings.

Every pilot trial uses the same deterministic sample of 50,000 training and
10,000 validation conformations. A pilot can train for at most 120 epochs, but
validation early stopping can finish it sooner.

Preview the selected trials without starting training:

python -m pharm_training.search \
  --config pharm_training/configs/spice_search.json \
  --device cuda \
  --dry-run

Start or resume the search:

python -m pharm_training.search \
  --config pharm_training/configs/spice_search.json \
  --device cuda

The search writes:

runs/pharm_training/spice_search/
  search_summary.csv
  best_config.json
  best_full_config.json
  trial_<id>/
    config.json
    console.log
    metrics.csv
    results.csv
    checkpoints/

Completed trials are skipped when the command is repeated. Interrupted trials
resume from last.pt. Failed trials remain visible in search_summary.csv and keep
their console logs for diagnosis.

best_config.json contains the winning pilot configuration. best_full_config.json
keeps its selected hyperparameters but restores the full dataset, test
evaluation, and the long training budget.


Long Full-Dataset Training
--------------------------

The full SPICE configuration allows up to 700 epochs. This is a maximum budget,
not a requirement to train for all 700 epochs.

The run uses the following safeguards:

- AdamW optimization;
- learning-rate reduction when validation performance plateaus;
- early stopping after 20 epochs without a meaningful validation improvement;
- a minimum of 25 epochs before divergence-based stopping;
- immediate failure on NaN or infinite losses;
- discard at most five isolated batches with non-finite parameter gradients per
  epoch, then stop with affected parameter names if instability is systematic;
- gradient clipping with a maximum norm of 10;
- severe-divergence stopping when the validation score becomes more than four
  times worse than the best score;
- last and best recovery checkpoints;
- archival epoch checkpoints every 25 epochs.

Run the baseline full configuration directly with:

python -m pharm_training.train \
  --config pharm_training/configs/spice.json \
  --device cuda

The preferred workflow is to run the search first and then train the promoted
winner:

python -m pharm_training.train \
  --config runs/pharm_training/spice_search/best_full_config.json \
  --device cuda

Rerunning the same command with the same output directory automatically resumes
from checkpoints/last.pt.

For final reporting, the winning configuration should be repeated with multiple
random seeds. Hyperparameter selection from one validation split identifies a
strong configuration, but it does not prove that the configuration is globally
optimal or independent of random initialization.


Training Outputs
----------------

A full run writes:

runs/pharm_training/spice/
  config.json
  metrics.csv
  results.csv
  logs/
  checkpoints/
    last.pt
    best.pt
    trained_encoder.pt
    epoch_0025.pt
    epoch_0050.pt
    ...

metrics.csv contains separate training and validation values for:

- total weighted loss;
- energy Smooth-L1 loss;
- force Smooth-L1 loss;
- energy MAE in eV/atom;
- force MAE in eV/Angstrom;
- normalized validation selection score;
- learning rate.

best.pt contains the complete potential, optimizer, scheduler, model
architecture, element list, and training configuration. trained_encoder.pt is
the smaller transfer checkpoint containing the geometric Equiformer core and its
architecture metadata.


Connection to Pharmacophore Screening
--------------------------------------

The SPICE-specific element embedding and the energy prediction head are not
transferred to pharmacophore screening. The transferable part is the Equiformer
geometric core.

This separation is necessary because the two stages use different input
modalities:

- SPICE pretraining starts from atomic element identities;
- pharmacophore screening starts from molecular descriptors and RDKit-derived
  feature definitions.

For screening, descriptor vectors are first projected to the hidden dimension
saved in the SPICE checkpoint. EquiformerAdjEncoder.encode_embedded_nodes() then
produces invariant atom embeddings using the pretrained geometric core.

SPICEPharmacophoreEncoder.from_pretrained() reads the saved architecture,
constructs the descriptor projection and matching EquiformerAdj core, and loads
only encoder_state_dict. This is the supported handoff from the SPICE training
workflow to future pharmacophore screening.

RDKit identifies pharmacophore features and supplies the atom IDs belonging to
each feature. encode_pharmacophore_features() averages the relevant atom
embeddings and returns:

- one learned embedding per pharmacophore feature;
- feature family and type;
- the atom IDs defining the feature;
- the 3D center of the feature.

These feature-level embeddings and metadata can be passed to the existing
Hungarian matching implementation. Hungarian assignment remains outside the
Equiformer model because matching is a downstream screening decision, not part
of quantum-potential pretraining.

The pretrained checkpoint is not, by itself, a finished pharmacophore matcher.
It must be connected to the descriptor input layer and fine-tuned or calibrated
for the pharmacophore objective before it is used for ligand ranking.


Reproducibility Checklist
-------------------------

1. Record the SPICE release and checksum.
2. Keep the generated molecule-disjoint manifest.
3. Preserve the search configuration and search_summary.csv.
4. Select hyperparameters only from validation results.
5. Evaluate the test split only after selecting the final configuration.
6. Preserve best.pt, trained_encoder.pt, config.json, metrics.csv, and
   results.csv.
7. Repeat the final configuration across multiple seeds before reporting the
   final error.
